Hello there! I am alive! Mostly! Hooooly mother of all Indies, people were not lying when they warned me that September is busy season for Tokyo games. I have run across three prefectures nearly every day the last two weeks for TGS and TGS prep, made two custom dresses and ran the biggest event of my life.

So lets get into the recap before I potato for the remainder of the month.

## Video Game Prom!

Who on EARTH agreed to make two custom dresses in like one week?? I need to speak to their manager.

![](/photos/tgs-september-2026/iridescent-fabric.png)

Yeah, that’s my fault. Also, fuck this fabric cause god damn it’s a BITCH to work with.

Anyway.

First thing’s first, me and my friend Lauren (check out their patreon [here](https://www.patreon.com/c/crown_of_ink/posts), they make the most adorable anxious boys in their novel Noah Arc) went to Nippori to hunt fabric for us both. As it was my first time in Nippori, I was basically a kid in a candy shop, grinning and bouncing around and generally just being super excited. I cannot express to you all how much I love fashion. Tokyo is a dream come true for me, the textures! The colors! The ACCESSORIES!! I just.. *scream* I love it, okay. Look at this. Tell me this isn’t cool as hell.

![](/photos/tgs-september-2026/nippori-accessories.jpeg)

Why, yes. I will be going back for these, thank you.

So we were originally looking for some fabric to make a cape/cloak style shoulder covering for a dress they already owned. Some cool ideas of a gameboy color inspired cape were tossed around and I found some nice fabric for it. And then… and then we saw the devil. And by the devil, I mean this fabric. The iridescent sheen called to me, the rainbow coloring… metallic. Stretchy. Slippery. All the worst qualities in a fabric, especially when you’re in crunch mode.

Naturally we bought 7 meters of it.

IN MY DEFENSE!

jk I have none, I was just mesmerized. Also we found this other fabric and saw this miniature dress form and I just.. I caved, okay? How could I not make a dress like this??

![](/photos/tgs-september-2026/miniature-dress-form.jpeg)

Sooo yeah, I made two dresses in a week. And by made, I mean literally took measurements, created custom patterns, and made an absolute mess of my office. Really it was more like two days because I was working three other jobs and made most of Lauren’s dress day of. But you know what, they still slapped so I count it as a win.

![](/photos/tgs-september-2026/prom-dress.jpeg)

![](/photos/tgs-september-2026/prom-dresses.jpeg)

I do believe the words “hot af” were uttered at least three times~

It was a lot of fun, regardless of the stress I felt making them! But, um… maybe next time I’ll not decide to attend and make dresses from scratch.

## Tokyo Game Show

![](/photos/tgs-september-2026/tokyo-game-show.jpeg)

Ohhh BOY was this insane. I have been to exactly 1 game convention before and it was PAX in Seattle. Which, post-covid was… lame, alright? I’m gonna be real, it was lame and boring and parking was a nightmare and there were like no games and it just kind of sucked. Tokyo Game Show (according to my partner) was the equivalent of E3 back in 2012 when they went. Love it or hate it, it was so much bigger than PAX. The booths were all very cool, and I’m glad I got to see it on the business days, but I spent most of my time running a booth in the Selected Indies for a game called In Silico! (psst, wishlist [here](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3620520/IN_SILICO/), it’s such a freaking cool game). It was a lot of fun watching everyone play it, seeing their reactions and taking feedback notes for the developer.

![Image](/photos/tgs-september-2026/in-silico-booth.webp)

I learned a lot from the experience and hope to someday show a game of my own on the same floor! I definitely think the Selected Indies area of TGS was the coolest part of the entire convention.

## Tokyo Game Bridge TGS Community Fest

Alright, first of all, this was the highlight of my month. Prom was great, Tokyo Game Show was a lot of fun, but this one event caps it all off for me. Why? Because it was mine! Well, not just mine. I worked hard with my fellow volunteers at Tokyo Game Bridge to make this event happen and it was a smashing success! We had over 150 people show, nearly 30 games presented, and everyone left with a smile on their face. It was the biggest event we ever ran, and I got to enjoy it with the knowledge that I helped make it happen.
